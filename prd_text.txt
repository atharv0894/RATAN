1. Executive Summary
What it is: A RAG-powered Industrial Knowledge Intelligence platform that ingestsheterogeneous plant documents (manuals, SOPs, P&IDs, work orders, incident reports,
inspection records) and a knowledge graph of assets/equipment/failures, and exposesboth through a cited, conversational AI Copilot plus an Asset 360 view.
Who it's for: Plant operators, maintenance/reliability engineers, ﬁeld technicians, quality
engineers, operations managers, and knowledge/enterprise admins at asset-intensiveindustrial sites (manufacturing, energy, process industries).
Problem it solves: Per the source material, professionals in asset-intensive industrieslose <cite index="0-1">an average of 35% of their working hours searching forinformation, clarifying instructions, or recreating documents that already exist
somewhere in the organisation</cite>. Indian plants speciﬁcally run <cite index="0-1">7
--- PAGE 2 ---
to 12 disconnected document systems</cite>, and this fragmentation is estimated todrive <cite index="0-1">18–22% of unplanned downtime</cite>. A retirement wave
threatens to erase undocumented tribal knowledge permanently.
Why existing search fails: Enterprise/document search returns ﬁles, not answers — it
has no concept of equipment, failure mode, or maintenance history as ﬁrst-class entities,no cross-document reasoning, no citation-grounded synthesis, and no way to detectthat a procedure is missing or outdated rather than simply "not indexed."
Core value proposition: One conversational entry point that answers operationalquestions the way a 20-year veteran engineer would — citing the exact manual page,
connecting a current fault to past incidents, and surfacing the right SOP — regardless ofwhich of the 7–12 original systems the answer lives in.
Expected impact: Reduced mean-time-to-answer for operational queries (target:
minutes →  seconds), reduced repeat/unplanned downtime attributable to missingfailure-history context, faster onboarding for new engineers, and audit-readycompliance evidence generation.
2. Problem Statement Analysis (Extracted, Not Invented)
Exact business problem: <cite index="0-1">Knowledge fragmentation in industrialoperations is not a ﬁle management problem. It is a safety problem, a quality problem,
and an operational eﬃciency problem — and it compounds over time.</cite>
Root causes (from source):
Documents scattered across <cite index="0-1">7 to 12 disconnected document
systems</cite> — P&IDs/drawings in one, work orders in another, SOPs in a third,inspection records in a fourth, regulatory submissions in email archives.
An impending knowledge cliff: <cite index="0-1">an estimated 25% of India'sexperienced industrial engineers and operators will retire within the nextdecade</cite>, taking undocumented operational knowledge with them,
permanently.
Current workﬂow (implied): An engineer facing an anomaly manually checks multiple
systems/binders/emails, asks colleagues, or recreates a document from memory — the35%-of-time sink described above.
Current pain points: No single place to ask a question; no linkage between a live fault
and historical failure/incident data; regulatory and quality evidence assembledmanually; knowledge never captured before an expert leaves.
Knowledge fragmentation issues: Same equipment's manual, maintenance history,SOP, and incident record live in different, unlinked systems with no shared identiﬁer(e.g., equipment tag) connecting them.
--- PAGE 3 ---
Operational risks: Decisions made <cite index="0-1">without complete equipmenthistory or failure pattern context</cite>, contributing directly to unplanned downtime;
safety risk from outdated/conﬂicting procedures; compliance risk from scatteredregulatory submissions.
Why traditional search fails: Keyword/ﬁle search returns documents ranked by termmatch, not by asset relevance, recency, or cross-document synthesis — it cannotanswer "what caused this before" or "is this procedure current."
Why industrial context matters: An answer is only useful if grounded to the exact asset,the exact revision of a manual, and traceable evidence — hallucinated or uncited
answers in a plant setting are a safety liability, not just an inconvenience.
Key constraints from the original problem statement:
Must ingest heterogeneous documents: <cite index="0-1">engineering drawings,

only.
Suggested (not mandated) technology directions: <cite index="0-2">RAG overheterogeneous industrial document corpora, Knowledge Graphs & Industrial

compliance workﬂows</cite>.
Expected deliverables: <cite index="0-2">Working Prototype, Architecture Diagram,Presentation Deck, Demo Video</cite>.
Evaluation focus: <cite index="0-2">entity extraction accuracy across documenttypes, query answer quality on domain-expert benchmark questions, knowledge

3. Product Vision
Vision statement: Every person on the plant ﬂoor gets a cited, trustworthy answer toany operational question in seconds, from any device.
--- PAGE 4 ---
Mission: Unify a plant's fragmented documents and tribal knowledge into one living,queryable "operations brain" before it's lost.
Long-term vision: An always-current digital twin of institutional knowledge — auto-updating as new work orders, incidents, and manuals arrive — that proactively ﬂags
gaps and risks before they cause downtime.
Hackathon MVP vision: Prove the core loop end-to-end on a realistic synthetic dataset:ingest →  extract entities/graph →  ask a hard cross-document question →  get a cited,
correct answer faster than manual search, plus one "wow" feature (similar-incidentdiscovery or knowledge-gap detection).
Product principles:
1. Never answer without a citation.
2. Assets, not documents, are the organizing unit.
3. Mobile/ﬁeld-usable by default, not a desktop afterthought.
4. Say "I don't know" over hallucinating in a safety-relevant domain.
5. Every gap the system ﬁnds is as valuable as every answer it gives.

7.1 Multi-Source Knowledge Ingestion
Supported formats (hackathon-realistic subset in bold; rest as stretch):PDF manuals,
SOPs (PDF/DOCX), CSV/Excel maintenance & work-order exports, scanned/imagedocuments (OCR); stretch: live DB/API connectors, structured drawing metadata.
Upload ﬂow: drag-drop or mobile camera capture →  client-side type/size validation→  upload to object storage →  job enqueued →  status polled/pushed to UI.
--- PAGE 7 ---
Batch ingestion: a "folder upload" mode accepts a zip or multi-ﬁle selection; eachﬁle becomes its own ingestion job.
Connectors (P2/stretch): generic REST/CSV pull connector stub — out of scope forhackathon beyond a mocked example.
Validation: ﬁle type allowlist, max size (e.g., 25MB), virus/malware scan stub,duplicate-hash check before processing.
Parsing: format-speciﬁc extractors (PDF text layer, DOCX via python-docx, XLSX via
openpyxl/pandas, images via OCR).
OCR: Tesseract (or PaddleOCR) applied to scanned pages/images; conﬁdence
score stored per page.
Metadata extraction: ﬁlename, upload date, document type (classiﬁed), detectedequipment tags, page count.
Deduplication: SHA-256 content hash; if matched, link to existing document insteadof re-ingesting.
Versioning: new upload with same detected title/asset supersedes prior version;
old version retained but ﬂagged "superseded."
Reprocessing: admin-triggered re-run of parse/OCR/embedding for a document
(e.g., after a pipeline bug ﬁx).
7.2 Industrial Knowledge Processing Pipeline (step-by-step)
1. Ingest raw ﬁle →  object storage.
2. Parse: extract raw text + layout (page/section boundaries); route scanned pages toOCR.
3. Table extraction: detect and extract tabular regions (e.g., maintenance schedules)into structured rows, not ﬂattened text.
4. Layout-aware extraction: preserve heading hierarchy so a "Section 4.2 ShutdownProcedure" stays attributable.
5. Chunking: semantic chunking within layout boundaries (see Section 17).
6. Metadata enrichment: attach document type, source, page number, section title toevery chunk.
7. Asset/equipment tag extraction: regex + NER pass to detect tags like "P-101", "C-204" (pattern: letter preﬁx + dash + number).
8. Entity extraction: equipment, failure modes, personnel/roles, dates, regulatory
references (via LLM structured extraction call).
9. Relationship extraction: LLM-assisted pass linking entities (e.g., "Pump P-101 →has_manual →  Doc123").
10. Embedding generation: vector embedding per chunk (see stack, Section 13).
11. Indexing: write to vector DB + keyword index + upsert graph nodes/edges.
7.3 Uniﬁed Asset & Operations Brain
--- PAGE 8 ---
The graph layer connects Assets ↔  Manuals ↔  SOPs ↔  Incidents ↔  Maintenancehistory ↔  Failure modes ↔  Work orders ↔  Locations ↔  People/roles ↔  Inspection
ﬁndings. Every document chunk, once entity-extracted, is linked to the graph node forthe equipment tag it mentions — so a query about "P-101" pulls from every document
type at once, not just one silo. This is the direct mechanism solving the "7–12disconnected systems" problem from the brief.
7.4 Hybrid RAG System
Retrieval ﬂow: query →  query rewriting (expand abbreviations/tags) →  parallel (a)vector search top-K, (b) BM25 keyword search top-K, (c) graph traversal from any
detected asset tag →  merge candidates →  rerank (cross-encoder or LLM-scored) →top-N context →  LLM generation with citation mapping.
Top-K strategy: vector K=20, BM25 K=20, graph neighbors depth-2; merged anddeduped before rerank to top 6–8 for the LLM context window.
Reranking: cross-encoder (e.g., bge-reranker) or, for hackathon speed, an LLM
relevance-scoring pass.
Context window management: truncate/compress low-relevance chunks; hard capcontext to ﬁt model window with room for the answer.
Hallucination reduction: system prompt forces "answer only from provided context;if not present, say so"; every claim must map to a retrieved chunk ID.
Conﬁdence scoring: derived from reranker score distribution + citation coverage (%of answer sentences with a matching source).
Fallback behavior: if conﬁdence below threshold, respond with best-effort answer
explicitly labeled "low conﬁdence — no strong source match" plus nearest relateddocuments, instead of a fabricated deﬁnitive answer.

7.8 Asset 360 View
Single page per equipment tag: header (name, tag, location, status), tabs for Manuals,

7.10 Knowledge Gap Detection
Rule + LLM-assisted checks: equipment nodes with zero linked SOP/manual →  ﬂagged

ﬂagged "unresolved recurring issue"; low-conﬁdence-answer queries logged as"knowledge gap candidates."

distribution, average AI conﬁdence trend, active-user adoption metrics.

conﬁguration, knowledge graph browser/editor, audit log viewer.

16. AI/RAG Technical Design — Pipeline Pseudocode
def answer_query(query, org_id, conversation_history):    rewritten = rewrite_query(query, conversation_history)       # expand tags/abbreviations
    asset_tags = extract_asset_tags(rewritten)                   # regex + NER
    vector_hits = vector_search(rewritten, org_id, k=20)
--- PAGE 19 ---
    keyword_hits = bm25_search(rewritten, org_id, k=20)    graph_hits = graph_traverse(asset_tags, depth=2) if asset_tags else []
    candidates = dedupe(vector_hits + keyword_hits + graph_hits)    reranked = rerank(query, candidates, top_n=8)
    context = build_context(reranked, max_tokens=6000)
    prompt = construct_prompt(query, context, citation_instructions=True)
    response = llm_generate(prompt)                              # Claude API call    citations = map_sentences_to_chunks(response, reranked)
    confidence = score_confidence(citations, reranked)
    if confidence < THRESHOLD:        response = flag_low_confidence(response)
    log_interaction(query, response, citations, confidence)    return {"answer": response, "citations": citations, "confidence": confidence}

18. Security and Enterprise Requirements
RBAC (role →  allowed actions/documents); organization isolation via org_id scopingon every query; document- and asset-level permission checks before retrieval;
encryption at rest (DB/object storage) and in transit (TLS); audit logs for allreads/writes on sensitive documents; secure ﬁle upload (type/size validation, malware
scan stub); prompt-injection defense (system prompt hardening + input sanitization,ignore instructions embedded inside retrieved documents); data leakage prevention(never let retrieved context from Org A appear in Org B's answers); PII
detection/redaction pass during ingestion.
19. Evaluation Framework
Retrieval: Precision@K, Recall@K, MRR, nDCG against a hand-labeled benchmark
query set (20–30 questions with known correct source documents).
Answer quality: relevance and faithfulness scored via LLM-as-judge againstretrieved context; citation accuracy = % of cited chunks that actually support the
claim.
Hallucination rate: % of answer sentences with no supporting citation.
Latency: p50/p95 end-to-end response time.
User satisfaction: thumbs up/down on each answer, tracked in analytics.
Test dataset strategy: build a small synthetic-but-realistic corpus (Section 24) plus
20–30 benchmark Q&A pairs with ground-truth source documents, usedconsistently pre/post any pipeline change.
20. MVP Scope for Hackathon
P0 (Must Have): document upload + parsing (PDF/DOCX/XLSX/scanned image), OCR,
chunking + embedding, equipment tag extraction, hybrid retrieval (vector+keyword),cited chat answers, Asset 360 basic view, mobile-responsive chat UI.
P1 (Should Have): knowledge graph (NetworkX) with graph-based retrieval boost,similar-incident discovery, knowledge-gap detection (undocumented assets), analyticsdashboard, admin ingestion monitor, basic RBAC.
P2 (Nice to Have): compliance evidence export, Neo4j-based graph explorer UI, rerankermodel (vs. simple score fusion), multi-org support, oﬄine/degraded-mode mobile

27. Competitive Differentiation
vs. traditional enterprise search: organizes around assets/equipment, not ﬁles;answers questions, doesn't just return document lists.
vs. basic document chatbots: cross-document, cross-system reasoning via theknowledge graph, not single-corpus Q&A.
vs. standard vector-only RAG: hybrid retrieval (vector + keyword + graph) catchesexact equipment-tag matches that pure semantic search misses.
vs. generic AI copilots: citation-gated, conﬁdence-scored, and mobile/ﬁeld-usable
by design for a safety-relevant domain.

GraphRAG (graph-augmented retrieval) — realistic for hackathon, core to this MVP.

Multimodal industrial intelligence (P&ID/drawing understanding via vision models)
— stretch/P2, mention as roadmap.
Predictive knowledge recommendations — stretch, describe as post-hackathonroadmap.
Failure pattern detection — realistic, simple frequency-based pattern mining isachievable in 72h.
Knowledge gap detection — realistic, rule-based version is core P1.
Asset digital thread — long-term vision, not a 72h build.
Human feedback loops (thumbs up/down improving retrieval) — realistic as a
logging mechanism, full learning loop is stretch.
